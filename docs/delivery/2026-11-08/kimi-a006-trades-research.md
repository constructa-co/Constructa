## 1. Fresh market pulse (retrieved 2026-11-08)

- **FMB/CIOB State of Trade Survey H2 2025** (covers Jul–Dec 2025, published ~Mar 2026): SME builders report positive workloads and enquiries "in the black" but slowing momentum; "nearly half of firms report job delays linked to a lack of skilled tradespeople" ([FMB resource page](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/state-of-trade-survey-h2-2025.html)). Secondary coverage puts skills-shortage impact at ~72% of firms, ~75% seeing material-cost increases in H2 2025, 48% positive outlook for H1 2026 ([Bricks & Bytes, 2026-04-02](https://bricks-bytes.com/news/uk-construction-growth-is-slowing/); [Professional Builders Merchant, 2026-04-10](https://professionalbuildersmerchant.co.uk/news/fmb-and-ciob-report-highlights-sustained-challenges-for-sme-builders/)). *Treat the percentages as single-source survey reporting until checked against the FMB PDF itself.*
- **Prior survey wave**: H1 2025 (published Sep 2025) showed workload/enquiries/employment improving, with 61% of construction SMEs affected by skills shortages ([Construction Management, 2025-09-02](https://constructionmanagement.co.uk/survey-finds-61-of-construction-smes-are-facing-skills-shortages/); [Build News, 2025-09-02](https://www.buildnews.co.uk/uk-sme-construction-firms-rekindled-workloads-in-early-2025/)). *Note the trend (61% → ~72%) suggests worsening, but the two waves were reported by different outlets — unverified as a trend.*
- **Macro output**: ONS construction output for Great Britain fell ~2.1% in December 2025 ([ONS via Insight DIY, 2026-02-24](https://www.insightdiy.co.uk/news/ons-construction-output-for-december-2025/16110.htm)); Q3 2025 output grew 0.1% with repair & maintenance (+0.6%) outpacing new work (−0.2%) ([ONS via Insight DIY, 2025-11-20](https://www.insightdiy.co.uk/news/ons-construction-output-grew-slightly-in-september-2025/15796.htm)). *Repair-and-maintenance resilience is a consistent pattern across both releases — relevant because R&M is the domestic SME builder's core work.*
- **Firm counts vary by definition**: IBISWorld counts ~98,104 UK "construction contractors" businesses (CAGR +1.3% 2020–2025) ([IBISWorld, 2025-12-19](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-kingdom/industry/construction-contractors/14629/)); a marketing-sector source cites ~370,770 VAT/PAYE-registered construction businesses ([Made for Builders](https://madeforbuilders.com/en-GB/trades/small-construction-firms/), undated). *These conflict because the scopes differ (SIC-filtered industry analysis vs all VAT/PAYE registrants). Do not cite either without stating the definition — see §4.*

## 2. What this means for Constructa's positioning (analysis, not facts)

- The consistent survey signal — workloads OK, enquiries softening, skills shortages causing delays, costs rising — describes exactly the firm that feels pricing/margin pressure and needs to win the jobs it quotes well. That supports (but does not prove demand for) the existing "fast, accurate proposals / built-in cost control" pillars.
- R&M outpacing new work favours content and vocabulary aimed at renovation/refurb/extension buyers, not only new-build.
- The buyer is time-poor and skills-constrained; any content pilot (A103) or newsletter (A101) must respect that tone.
