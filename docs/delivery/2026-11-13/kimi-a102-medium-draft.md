## Medium draft — "The proposal is a package, not a price"

**Status: DRAFT · NOT APPROVED · NOT PUBLISHED**

---

A client rarely rejects a proposal because one line was a pound out. More often the doubts are quieter: *are these dates real? does this cover what we actually agreed? what happens after I say yes?*

The honest answer is that a proposal is a package, not a price. The number only holds up if three other things hold up with it: the brief behind it, the programme around it, and the terms that come with it.

**Start with the brief.** The brief is the job in your own words — the trades involved, access, restrictions, assumptions and a likely start date. The price built on it should be one-price items with quantities and rates, shown as price lines with a running total, with exclusions and clarifications kept with the job. The check is simple to say and easy to skip: are the scope, quantities, rates and total the ones you intend to stand behind?

**Then the programme.** Dates are part of the offer. A target start date — or working days from Day 1 if the start is not fixed — plus how long the job takes and any stages you want the client to see. A programme at a glance, with a finish date or a finish day. If the stage order would not survive contact with the actual job, the proposal is saying something you do not mean.

**Then the terms — read the whole preview.** Payment stages, how many days the price stays valid, and the terms for this proposal specifically. Read the on-screen preview end to end, not just the total.

One more habit worth keeping: the draft PDF is marked as a draft, and downloading it sends nothing to the client. Nothing is "sent" until you choose to share it. And when a client does respond, the response options ask for a receipt or a non-binding intention to proceed — neither is acceptance. Knowing that in advance saves an awkward conversation later.

These checks come from Constructa's review checklists, written for people using an existing review account in the phase-one preview — working notes for checking your own work, not a claim that every held feature is available.
