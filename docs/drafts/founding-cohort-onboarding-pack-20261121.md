## Help: write a useful brief

A brief is the plain-English description of the job. Start with what the client wants, where the work is and the scope you expect to price. Separate facts from assumptions. Note what is excluded, what still needs measuring and which client decisions could change cost or timing. Avoid presenting an uncertain allowance as an agreed commitment.

Save your changes, reopen the project and check that the intended brief is present. If it is missing or different, stop relying on that version and report the affected step without copying sensitive client information into support email. [ROB TO CONFIRM: verified brief navigation or help link].

## Help: build and check an estimate

Use your own labour, material and other costs. Check quantities, units and rates line by line; an organised estimate still needs your judgment. Review adjustments, tax treatment and the resulting total against the figures you intended before sending anything to a client. Do not treat a missing cost as free work.

Save and reopen the estimate to check the intended version. If the displayed total or saved content differs from what you expect, resolve it before sharing. ## Help: work with a programme

A programme describes the proposed order and timing of work. Check dependencies, working days, site access, supplier lead times and client decisions against the real job. Suggested sequencing and dates are starting points, not commitments made to the client or subcontractors.

Review changes deliberately before using them in a proposal. Save, reopen and verify the intended version using [ROB TO CONFIRM: verified programme save/review guidance]. Do not assume a draft has automatically changed a client-facing proposal. If a change is not retained, stop and report it. ## Help: prepare a proposal

A proposal brings together the scope, estimate and other project information you intend to share. Read the complete document, not just its headline total. Check client/project details, inclusions and exclusions, amounts, dates, payment stages and terms for contradictions. The terms you agree with your client govern the work; domestic wording must not silently be treated as suitable for a commercial client. Obtain appropriate qualified advice for legal terms.
