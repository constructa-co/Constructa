## Working days and calendar days are not the same thing

On Programme you save working days, dependencies and a start date. The chart spans calendar weeks. Before you promise a finish week to a client, read the working calendar the job uses: site access, bank holidays, your own working pattern and anything that blocks productive days. A stage duration in **working** days is not the same as how long the bar looks on the timeline — check the dates the planner shows for each stage, not only the number in the Days column.

## Overlapping stages — read dates, do not stack durations

Walk the sequence stage by stage. Where trades overlap in real life, look at the **scheduled start and finish dates**, not at adding every stage duration into one long chain. Use the finish-to-start dependencies you set and ask whether they match how the job will actually be delivered. If two activities can run together, the plan should show that overlap in dates; if they cannot, the dependency should force a realistic handover.

## Final-week marker — reminder, not critical path

A stage marked as finishing in the final week is a visual reminder on long programmes. It is **not** a critical-path calculation. The marker does not tell you float, slack, or the commercial consequence of every delay. If a stage slips, review what it affects — access, follow-on trades, client handovers, time-related prelims — yourself.

## Programme changes do not reprice prelims for you

Extending or shortening the job changes how long you are on site, but Constructa does not automatically revisit time-related preliminary lines on Estimating when you edit the programme. If durations shift, open the estimate, find prelims tied to time or attendance, and adjust them manually. Then re-read scope, programme and proposal together so priced work still matches the plan you intend to show.

## Read it like the client or site lead will

Finally, read the programme as someone else would: does the order make sense? Are durations credible for your crew and suppliers? Does the plan include work that has not been priced? Keep estimate, programme and proposal consistent through your own review — the guides are checklists, not automatic governance.

When timing is aligned with the price you checked, read the proposal preview the same way. Downloading a draft PDF sends nothing to the client. Client response options ask for receipt or a non-binding intention to proceed, and neither is acceptance.
