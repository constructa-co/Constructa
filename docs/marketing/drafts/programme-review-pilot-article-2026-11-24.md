# Labour hours are not a finish date

**Proposed, Rob to review — unpublished**

Kimi draft, 28 November 2026; R1 editorial review. Revision of the preserved A103 pilot, not a new public guide route.

Labour hours describe effort, not elapsed time. The same amount of work can take different periods to complete depending on when it can start, how many people can work together and what must happen between tasks. A finish date therefore needs a programme review, not just a labour total.

Manual durations and reviewed programme suggestions can help structure that review. A suggestion does not replace judgement or establish a critical-path assessment.

## Start with the calendar

Confirm the intended start and the working calendar before discussing the finish. Consider normal workdays, shifts, site closures, restricted hours and known holidays. Check whether different areas or activities follow different calendars.

An available day is not necessarily a fully productive day. Allow for inductions, access arrangements, setting up, coordination and other conditions that affect the time available for the work.

## Set out dependencies

Identify what must happen before each activity can start. Dependencies may include earlier trades, inspections, room clearance, client decisions, material deliveries or making services available.

Some tasks can run alongside others; some cannot. Space limits, safety arrangements and shared access may prevent work that looks concurrent on a list. Review suggested durations and sequences against these practical constraints.

## Check crew capacity

Labour effort does not divide neatly across any number of operatives. Some tasks suit a larger crew, while others are limited by workspace, supervision or the sequence of operations. Adding people may therefore add cost without shortening the duration.

A minimum paid gang is also different from productive crew capacity. A minimum attendance charge may apply even when the available work is below that level; it does not create additional productive hours.

Where stages overlap, do not duplicate a shared crew in the available capacity. Count the crew once and combine the demands placed on it where appropriate. If the planned hours available are below the hours needed, review the allocation or programme rather than assuming the dates will hold.

## Review suppliers and plant

Check supplier lead times, delivery arrangements and the availability of specified materials or fittings. Client-supplied items need the same review: their presence on a list does not establish when they will be ready for use.

For hired plant, distinguish the on-hire date, planned off-hire date, supplier charging end, notice requirements and collection arrangements. The supplier’s declared billing basis and minimum charges may affect cost even after productive use ends.

A planned off-hire date is not an off-hire request or supplier confirmation. It does not mean that anyone has called, emailed, booked or cancelled the hire. These are checks for the contractor; this article does not claim that the held programme-cost drafts are live product features.

## Recheck cost after programme changes

Changing the programme in the current product does not automatically reprice preliminaries. Review time-related items such as supervision, site facilities and plant against the revised duration, and check the cost implications before changing a price or issuing a revised proposal.

### Programme checklist

- Start point and working calendars confirmed
- Dependencies and working-space limits reviewed
- Durations checked against practical crew sizes
- Shared crews counted once across overlapping stages
- Supplier deliveries and client-supplied items aligned
- Plant charging, notice and collection terms understood
- Time-related costs reviewed after programme changes

**Practical read-through:** Follow the programme in order and, for each stage, ask what must be ready, who is available and what happens next. Then read it again by resource, checking each crew and item of plant across all overlapping work before relying on the finish date.
